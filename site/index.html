HARLAN, Iowa — A routine evening chore on a family farm in western Iowa ended in tragedy this week when a 68-year-old farmer was fatally attacked by a black bull in his own cattle pen, authorities said.

Marvin E. Thompson was found dead in the pen on his property near Harlan after he failed to return to the house for supper on Monday evening, according to the Shelby County Sheriff’s Office. His wife discovered him after he did not come in, deputies said.

Shelby County Sheriff Neil Gross said Thompson died from injuries caused by an attack from a large black bull that was part of the herd. Deputies secured the animal at the scene without further incident, the sheriff said.

“This was a sudden and violent encounter with an animal these families work around every single day,” the sheriff’s office indicated in describing the case, which it characterized as the kind of routine farm work that turned deadly in an instant. Investigators said there were no immediate prior signs of aggression documented in the statements available to them.

The timeline released by deputies is stark in its ordinariness. Thompson went out to tend the herd, as he had countless times before. When he did not come back for the evening meal, his wife went looking — and found him in the pen with the bull still nearby.

Authorities have not reported any indication that the bull had shown aggression toward Thompson or others before Monday, and no charges or further action against the animal have been announced. The bull remains secured following the events, the sheriff’s office said.

A hidden hazard on America’s farms

The death is a reminder of a danger that rarely makes national headlines but is well known across farm country: bulls, which can weigh well over a ton, are among the most dangerous animals routinely handled on cattle operations. Agricultural-safety specialists have long warned that even docile, familiar animals can turn without warning, particularly in the close quarters of a pen.

For the community around Harlan — a town of a few thousand people roughly an hour northeast of Omaha, Nebraska — the loss lands close to home in a place where livestock work is part of daily life.

The Shelby County Sheriff’s Office confirmed the black bull as the animal involved and said the investigation into the circumstances of Thompson’s death is continuing. This is a developing story.
